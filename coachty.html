Project Documentation

Discussion points that should be understood before any build begins.

This section is designed to reduce ambiguity and identify issues that require professional, technical, or client decisions before any future agreement.

1. Product Goals

  • Create a warm digital environment for structured emotional reflection.
  • Extend a trusted helper’s support without suggesting that helper is available in real time.
  • Help users notice recurring triggers, thoughts, emotions, and behaviors.
  • Keep AI suggestions optional, transparent, educational, and non-diagnostic.
  • Give users meaningful control over helper relationships and data access.

2. Actions Required Before Development

  • Confirm intended users and exactly who qualifies as a “helper.”
  • Determine whether HelperVoice is positioned as wellness, coaching support, clinical support, or another category.
  • Client responsibility: obtain and pay for qualified legal, privacy, regulatory, clinical, licensing, HIPAA, and compliance review applicable to the client’s intended use.
  • Define consent, revocation, retention, deletion, helper-access, and crisis-response policies.
  • Confirm technical architecture and vendor requirements before handling sensitive data.

3. Core Product Requirements

  • Transcripts or accessible alternatives for audio.
  • Clear opt-in for helper connections and AI-assisted analysis.
  • Ability to disconnect a helper and revoke future access.
  • Role-based authorization for client/helper/admin accounts.
  • Secure handling of recordings, reflections, identifiers, and account information.
  • Clear error, revoked-access, unavailable-audio, and empty states.

4. Viable / Lower-Risk Feature Concepts

  • Structured journaling and reflection.
  • User-generated private voice notes.
  • Client-authorized helper recordings.
  • Personal weekly summaries.
  • Educational thought-pattern explanations.
  • Helper invitation and consent management.
  • Accessibility controls and transcripts.

5. High-Risk Areas

  • AI interpreting crisis, trauma, or clinically sensitive statements.
  • AI-generated content being perceived as therapy, diagnosis, or licensed professional advice.
  • Helpers accessing reflections without clear permission.
  • Storage/transmission of sensitive voice, behavioral, or mental-health-related information.
  • Creating the impression that a helper is monitoring the client live.
  • Automated conclusions about diagnosis, mental state, dangerousness, or intent.
  • Emergency, self-harm, abuse, or harm-to-others disclosures without an appropriate response framework.

6. HIPAA / Privacy / Compliance Questions

  • Will HelperVoice be direct-to-consumer or provided through clinicians/covered entities?
  • Could the platform create, receive, maintain, or transmit protected health information?
  • Would service providers need Business Associate Agreements?
  • What information is retained, for how long, and who can export/delete it?
  • How will recordings, transcripts, logs, backups, and analytics be classified and protected?
  • Which federal, state, consumer-health-data, professional licensing, and privacy rules apply?

Client responsibility: The client is responsible for determining the legal and regulatory requirements applicable to the intended business model and for obtaining qualified professional advice. OptiMaxProAI does not act as the client’s attorney, compliance officer, clinician, insurer, or regulator. Nothing in this prototype constitutes a compliance determination or guarantee.

7. AI Safety Requirements

  • Use tentative language such as “may reflect” rather than declaring conclusions.
  • Allow the user to disagree with or dismiss AI suggestions.
  • Avoid language implying surveillance, certainty, diagnosis, or clinical authority.
  • Separate educational guidance from professional advice.
  • Define fallback behavior when confidence is low or content is high-risk.

8. Scope, Approval & Change Control

  • No feature is approved merely because it appears in this mockup.
  • Only items specifically included in a later written agreement or statement of work should be considered part of any build.
  • New requests after scope approval may affect price, timeline, architecture, compliance, and feasibility.
  • Verbal comments, calls, texts, prototype discussions, and informal suggestions should not override a later signed written scope.
  • Regulated, therapeutic, diagnostic, crisis-response, or PHI-related functionality requires separate review before implementation.
  • OptiMaxProAI may recommend redesign, delay, removal, or refusal of a requested feature when significant legal, security, ethical, technical, or platform risk is identified.

9. Decisions Still Needed

  • Who can qualify as a helper?
  • Can a client have multiple helpers?
  • What, if anything, may helpers see from client reflections?
  • Is helper consent granted per relationship or per message?
  • Is Coach Ty the default guide, optional persona, or client-specific brand element?
  • What belongs in MVP versus later phases?
  • Who pays: client, helper, practice, employer, organization, or another party?
